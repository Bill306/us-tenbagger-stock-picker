# US Tenbagger Stock Picker

## Purpose

This skill evaluates whether a US-listed company has potential to become a long-term multibagger, especially a possible tenbagger.

It combines two frameworks:

1. Quantitative tenbagger traits: small market cap, valuation mismatch, profitability, free cash flow, reinvestment quality, momentum behavior, and macro liquidity.
2. Structural compounding models: free-cash-flow-per-share inflection, Costco-style shared-scale business models, and NVDA-style technology-breakthrough models.

Do not treat this skill as investment advice. Use it to build a research thesis, identify risks, and decide what data must be verified.

## Initial Response

When invoked without a ticker or company, ask the user for:

- Ticker or company name
- Investment horizon
- Whether they want a quick screen or deep research

If the user asks for a general framework, summarize the checklist and explain how to apply it.

## Core Principle

A true tenbagger usually comes from a combination of:

```text
Low starting base
+ improving business quality
+ free cash flow inflection
+ long reinvestment runway
+ structural growth driver
+ market under-recognition
+ favorable or improving liquidity cycle
```

Avoid relying on one metric alone. EPS growth, revenue growth, AI narrative, or price momentum by itself is not enough.

## Tenbagger Screening Checklist

### 1. Starting Market Cap

Prefer companies that begin from a smaller market cap.

Strong signals:

- Small or mid-cap company
- Large total addressable market
- Market cap still small relative to future opportunity
- Not already priced as a dominant mega-cap winner

Weak signals:

- Already one of the largest companies in the market
- Requires unrealistic valuation expansion to 10x
- Growth story is already consensus

Key question:

```text
Can this company plausibly become 5-10x larger without requiring absurd market assumptions?
```

### 2. Valuation Mismatch

Look for companies where market price does not yet reflect business value.

Positive signs:

- High book-to-market or asset value mismatch
- Depressed valuation due to temporary fear
- Business quality improving faster than investor perception
- Market still categorizes the company incorrectly

Examples of misclassification:

- "Government contractor" when it is becoming a software platform
- "Hardware company" when it is becoming AI infrastructure
- "Low-margin retailer" when it is building a trust and membership flywheel

Key question:

```text
What does the market currently misunderstand?
```

### 3. Profitability Quality

A potential tenbagger does not always need high current EPS growth, but it should show improving economic quality.

Check:

- ROE trend
- Gross margin stability
- Operating margin direction
- Net income quality
- Unit economics
- Customer retention
- Pricing power or cost advantage

Strong signal:

```text
Revenue growth is converting into better margins, better cash flow, or stronger unit economics.
```

Weak signal:

```text
Growth requires constant dilution, debt, or unsustainable marketing spend.
```

### 4. Free Cash Flow Inflection

Free cash flow is central.

Prioritize:

- Free cash flow turning positive
- Free cash flow per share growing
- FCF margin expanding
- FCF yield becoming attractive
- Share dilution slowing
- Operating leverage appearing

Important distinction:

```text
Total FCF growth is good.
FCF per share growth is better.
```

A company can grow cash flow but still destroy per-share value if dilution is excessive.

Key question:

```text
Is each share entitled to more future free cash flow over time?
```

### 5. Reinvestment Quality

Tenbaggers usually reinvest well.

Look for capital expenditure and investment that match business expansion.

Healthy pattern:

- Capex supports future EBITDA growth
- R&D creates durable product or technical advantage
- Sales and marketing spend produces sticky customers
- Reinvestment does not permanently suppress cash flow

Danger pattern:

- Revenue grows only because spending grows faster
- Capex is aggressive but returns are unclear
- Management constantly raises capital without operating leverage
- Growth looks impressive but per-share economics deteriorate

Key question:

```text
Is the company reinvesting into a compounding engine or just buying temporary growth?
```

### 6. Costco Model

Use this model for companies that build long-term value by sharing scale advantages with users.

The company may sacrifice short-term profit to improve customer value, trust, retention, and frequency.

Positive signs:

- Lower prices or better service as the company scales
- High customer retention
- Increasing usage frequency
- Strong word of mouth
- Improving fulfillment, delivery, software, or service efficiency
- Gross profit dollars grow even if margins stay restrained
- Customer value improves over time

Classic pattern:

```text
Scale advantage -> better user value -> higher retention -> more scale -> stronger cash flow
```

Examples of Costco-style logic:

- Costco: low prices and membership trust
- Amazon: Prime, logistics, cloud infrastructure
- Spotify: content, personalization, subscription scale
- Tesla: vertical integration, software, charging network
- HIMS-style model: direct-to-consumer healthcare, verticalized service chain

Key question:

```text
Does scale make the product cheaper, better, faster, or more trusted for users?
```

### 7. NVDA Model

Use this model for companies where long-term technical accumulation can suddenly become economically explosive.

Positive signs:

- 10+ years of technical development
- Proprietary infrastructure, architecture, data, software, or ecosystem
- Market has not fully understood the technical advantage
- A major industry shift unlocks demand
- Productization turns technical depth into revenue
- Free cash flow per share begins to inflect after the technology is adopted

Classic pattern:

```text
Long technical accumulation -> market underestimation -> external adoption trigger -> platformization -> FCF per share explosion
```

Examples of NVDA-style logic:

- NVIDIA: CUDA and GPU ecosystem before AI demand exploded
- AMD: Zen architecture and CPU efficiency reset
- Palantir-style model: long-built data platform becoming productized through AI demand

Key question:

```text
Is this company sitting on a technical asset that becomes much more valuable because the world changed?
```

### 8. Data and AI Flywheel

For software, platform, fintech, healthcare, marketplace, or AI companies, evaluate whether data compounds.

Positive signs:

- More users improve the product
- More usage creates proprietary data
- Data improves model, recommendation, automation, or workflow quality
- Better product increases retention
- Retention increases future data density

Flywheel:

```text
Users -> usage -> data -> better product -> retention -> more users
```

Weak signal:

```text
The company uses AI as marketing language but has no proprietary data, workflow ownership, or distribution advantage.
```

### 9. Momentum and Drawdown Behavior

Tenbaggers often show strong momentum but violent drawdowns.

Do not assume a strong chart means low risk.

Check:

- Has the stock already moved too far ahead of fundamentals?
- Are pullbacks caused by business deterioration or valuation reset?
- Is the thesis still intact after drawdowns?
- Is the position size appropriate for volatility?

Rule:

```text
A tenbagger candidate can fall 30-70% on the way up.
```

The goal is not to avoid volatility. The goal is to distinguish volatility from thesis breakage.

### 10. Macro Liquidity

Macro conditions affect the timing of returns.

Favorable backdrop:

- Falling or low interest rates
- Improving liquidity
- Risk appetite returning
- Capital available for growth companies
- Inflation and discount-rate pressure easing

Unfavorable backdrop:

- Aggressive rate hikes
- Tight liquidity
- Long-duration growth stocks being repriced
- High valuation companies losing multiple support

Key question:

```text
Is the macro environment helping or fighting the valuation of long-duration growth?
```

## Scoring Framework

Score each category from 0 to 3.

| Category | Score |
| --- | --- |
| Small starting market cap | 0-3 |
| Valuation mismatch | 0-3 |
| Profitability quality | 0-3 |
| FCF per share inflection | 0-3 |
| Reinvestment quality | 0-3 |
| Costco model fit | 0-3 |
| NVDA model fit | 0-3 |
| Data or AI flywheel | 0-3 |
| Momentum with manageable drawdowns | 0-3 |
| Macro liquidity support | 0-3 |

Interpretation:

| Total Score | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| 24-30 | Strong tenbagger candidate, worth deep research |
| 18-23 | Promising but needs more evidence |
| 12-17 | Watchlist only |
| Below 12 | Weak tenbagger setup |

Never buy solely based on the score. Use the score to decide whether deeper research is justified.

## Red Flags

Be skeptical if:

- The company has no path to free cash flow
- Share dilution is extreme
- Growth depends mainly on hype
- Management constantly changes the story
- Revenue growth does not improve unit economics
- The company has no durable customer retention
- AI claims lack proprietary data or workflow ownership
- Valuation already assumes perfect execution
- Insider selling is heavy while fundamentals are weak
- The business needs easy money to survive

## Final Rule

A real tenbagger thesis must answer three questions:

```text
Why can this company become much larger?
Why has the market not fully priced it in?
Why will per-share value compound instead of being diluted away?
```

If any of these cannot be answered clearly, the stock is not yet a high-quality tenbagger candidate.
